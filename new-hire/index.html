Module 4

How we sell: relationships, not transactions

Clients buy from people they trust. Our edge is being the person a tech leader calls first, because you were useful before you ever asked for anything.

Trust over overselling

Promise what we can deliver, then deliver it. Say no to a role we can’t fill well. A credible “not this one” earns the next three.

Be useful every time

Bring something to every touch: a market insight, a salary data point, a relevant case study, a name.

Show up in person

On-site meetings build relationships faster than anything else. Your scorecard tracks in-person and virtual visits separately for a reason.

Steady cadence

Relationships are built on rhythm. A list without a cadence is just a list.

A simple cadence

ListTouchWhat a good touch looks like
Top 20 / A listEvery week or twoA call, a visit, or something useful sent their way. In person at least monthly.
B listEvery three to four weeksA relevant note tied to something happening at their company.
ProspectsWhen there is a triggerA short, specific reason to talk now. No generic “checking in.”
These are starting points. Agree your exact cadence with your manager and keep it in Salesforce so nothing slips.

Go wide inside every account

The Director or Sr Director who owns the work the trigger affects. They feel the pain and usually make the call.

Call second

Why Directors first: they own the work and feel the gap, and they are easier to reach than the C-suite. Bring the sponsor in once you understand the problem.
Module 5

Finding the reason to call: sales triggers

A trigger is a dated, public event that creates technology work. It turns a cold call into a relevant one: “I saw you just announced X. That usually means Y. How are you handling it?”

What counts as a trigger

Infrastructure modernization

Data center moves, network upgrades, end-user refreshes.

Cloud transformation

Migrations, new cloud platforms, cost programs.

Cybersecurity

Breaches, new compliance deadlines, a new CISO.

ERP & business systems

SAP, Oracle, Workday, NetSuite, Salesforce, ServiceNow rollouts or migrations.

AI & data

New AI programs, data platforms, analytics teams.

Application modernization

Re-platforming, new digital products, legacy rewrites.

Business events that drive tech

Acquisitions, new owners, a new CIO, CTO or CFO, funding, expansion, restructuring, or a burst of tech hiring in one area.

Which triggers matter most

  1. Acquisitions, mergers and ownership changesSystems have to be integrated or separated, fast.
  2. New technology leadershipNew CIOs and CTOs reshape teams and vendors in their first 6 to 12 months. A new CFO often reopens finance systems.
  3. System rolloutsERP, cloud, data and AI programs need people.
  4. Cyber and compliance deadlinesA dated deadline creates urgency.
  5. Other go-lives, funding and expansionGood, but usually less urgent.

Job postings count less on their own. A cluster of new postings in one tech area (say, five data engineers in a week) points to a project and counts as a trigger. Scattered postings are just supporting evidence.

The rules

  • Every trigger has an exact date and a source you opened. No date or no link means it is a lead, not a trigger.
  • Fresher is better. A trigger from the last six months is worth the most; after two years it is background.
  • Trust the source. A company press release or filing beats a single second-hand mention.
  • Never invent. If the evidence is thin, say so. Wrong facts end relationships.

Turn a trigger into a call

  1. What happenedThe fact, with the date.
  2. What it means for techWhich tech function it hits and why that creates work.
  3. Who owns itThe Director who feels it (buyer) and the VP or C-level above them (sponsor).
  4. How we helpOne capability that fits, or simply great people.
  5. The openerOne sentence tying the trigger to what that person owns, then a question.
Example: “I saw you announced the acquisition of [company] last week. Integrations like that usually land on the applications team first. Who on your side is owning the systems integration, and do you have the people for it?”
You don’t have to do this research alone. Every Monday the Northeast Tech Top Accounts app and the internal territory map are refreshed with new triggers, buyers, sponsors and new tech job openings. Start your week there.

Module 7

Running the meeting and writing the job

A great meeting is mostly listening. A great job order is the difference between filling the role and wasting everyone’s week.

Before the meeting

  • Know their latest trigger, their tech leaders and any open tech roles.
  • Know our history with them (KARE status, past placements, MSA).
  • Bring one useful thing: a salary data point from the 2027 Salary Guide or a relevant case study.

Questions that open things up

Priorities

“What has to go right for your team this year?”

The gap

“Where are you short on people or skills right now?”

The trigger

“How is [the event] changing your team’s workload?”

The process

“How do you usually bring in outside help, and who signs off?”

The past

“What worked, or didn’t, with partners before?”

Next step

“Who else should I be talking to about this?”

A complete job order

Close the candidate before you submit

Every candidate goes through a closing conversation before their résumé goes to a client. Confirm their motivation, the pay they will accept, their availability, other interviews and offers, the location and schedule, and that they say yes to this specific role. Surprises at offer stage almost always come from skipping this step.

Module 8

Pricing and gross margin

Gross margin (GM) is how we measure the value of every deal and how you get paid. Learn to price confidently.

The basics

Contract GM

Bill rate minus the consultant’s fully burdened pay (pay rate plus taxes, insurance and benefits), times the hours worked.

Margin vs markup

Margin is GM as a share of the bill rate. Markup is the bill rate over the pay rate. The same deal shows different numbers, so always say which one you mean.

Perm GM

On a direct hire, the placement fee is the GM.

Where our contract margins should land

MarginWhat it means
Under 25%Acceptable when the pay rate is higher
25% to 30%Decent
30% to 35%Where we want to be
35% to 40%That’s high: double-check the math, then great job
Use the tools: the Gross Margin Calculator works out burden, bill rate, margin and markup for you, and the Global Delivery rate tool prices offshore and nearshore options.
